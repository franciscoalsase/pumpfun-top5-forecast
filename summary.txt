World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026

Ukraine: Two days of US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi ended with an agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange; no ceasefire or deal on territory was reached (AP, Reuters).

Gulf: Saudi Arabia said it intercepted seven Houthi missiles and drones; a Houthi claim to have hit an oil pumping station at Yanbu was unconfirmed. Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei said no enriched nuclear material would leave Iran. Brent crude settled up 1.39% at $92.76 (all Reuters).

Gaza: Israeli fire killed at least seven people, including a father and son, 13, in a Gaza City shelter; the army said it struck militants (Reuters).

Spain: The first general strike in 14 years, called by unions CCOO and UGT over housing costs, cancelled 289 flights by 14:00 (Aena via EFE). In Madrid the government counted 120,000 marchers and organisers claimed 400,000; over 70 people were arrested nationwide (elDiario.es).

Climate: At the COP31 leaders' summit in Antalya, Turkey, UN chief Antonio Guterres said slow action is leading toward "mutually assured destruction" (Reuters).

Health: Congo's Ebola outbreak became the deadliest in its history, with over 11,000 cases and more than 5,000 deaths (Euronews).

Europe: At least 300 Italian schools were evacuated after bomb-threat emails, presumed hoaxes; at least four stray drones entered Baltic airspace and NATO scrambled jets (Reuters). The European Commission proposed a Digital Fairness Act with fines of up to 6% of global turnover (Euronews).

United States: AP called Mississippi's Senate race for Democrat Scott Colom over Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith. Prosecutors charged three alleged Iran-backed operatives over plots including one at Britain's RAF Fairford; Iran denies involvement (BBC).

Caribbean: Trinidad and Tobago's final court of appeal struck down its colonial-era ban on gay sex (AP).

Business: Anthropic plans an IPO raising up to $64 billion (Euronews).
